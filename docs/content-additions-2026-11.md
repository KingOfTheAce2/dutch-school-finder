# Content additions for expat and international families, November 2026

Ready to paste. Plain English, short sentences, no dashes. Each item ends with its source links. Facts checked on 2026-11-10. Where a point comes from a secondary source it says so in the text. Fee figures change every year, so the FAQ tells parents to ask the school.

## A. New FAQ entries

### 1. What is the difference between a Dutch school, an international school and a bilingual school?

A Dutch school teaches in Dutch and follows the Dutch curriculum. It is paid for by the state, and there are no tuition fees. An international school teaches in English or another language and often follows a programme such as the IB. Dutch International Schools are partly government funded and meant for children of internationally mobile families. Fully private international schools set their own fees. A bilingual school (tto) is a Dutch secondary school where at least half of the lessons in the first years of havo and vwo are taught in English. Pupils take the normal Dutch exams. Bilingual primary schools exist as a small pilot. Your choice depends on how long you will stay, your child's age and which language your child needs later.

Sources: [Nuffic, education in the Netherlands](https://www.nuffic.nl/en/education-systems/netherlands) · [Rijksoverheid, what is tto](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/vraag-en-antwoord/onderwijs-en-internationalisering/wat-is-tweetalig-onderwijs-tto-in-het-voortgezet-onderwijs) · [Dutch International Schools](https://www.dutchinternationalschools.nl/) · [SIO](https://www.sio.nl/)

### 2. What do international schools cost?

Dutch schools funded by the state do not charge tuition. International schools do. Private international schools set their own fees. For 2026-27, one Amsterdam school (ISA) publishes tuition from about 22,800 euro a year in nursery to about 32,800 euro in the last two years. Some schools add other fees, such as a deposit. Dutch International Schools are partly government funded and usually cost less. Third-party guides report about 4,500 to 9,500 euro a year, but we have not checked this against school fee schedules. Fees and admission rules differ per school and change every year. Ask each school for its current fee schedule and for the rules on who can apply.

Sources: [ISA fees 2026-27](https://www.isa.nl/fees/) · [Dutch International Schools](https://www.dutchinternationalschools.nl/) · [IamExpat, school fees (third-party guide)](https://www.iamexpat.nl/education/primary-secondary-education/tuition-fees-netherlands)

### 3. Can my child join a Dutch school without speaking Dutch?

Yes. What is on offer depends on age. In primary school, newcomers can join a language class or a transition class (taalklas or schakelklas), or a regular class with extra Dutch lessons. In secondary school, pupils from age 12 who speak little or no Dutch can join an international transition class (ISK). Pupils learn Dutch and prepare for regular lessons. Many stay up to about two years, then move to a regular class. Places can be limited in some cities, so ask early. Ask your municipality or a nearby school where places exist. Some schools also run special programmes for non-Dutch speakers, but bilingual schools can be hard for older children who start without Dutch.

Sources: [Rijksoverheid, newcomer education](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/basisonderwijs/onderwijs-voor-nieuwkomers) · [Nuffic, education in the Netherlands](https://www.nuffic.nl/en/education-systems/netherlands)

### 4. We arrive in the middle of the school year. Can my child still start?

Yes. Children can register at any time of the year. Secondary school registration also works at any time if you move house. The school may have no places, so register at more than one school if you can. Special support or a place in a newcomer class can take time. For children who need extra support, register at least 10 weeks before the start date, in writing, and tell the school about the support right away. The school then has 6 weeks to decide, and can extend this once by up to 4 weeks. Bring your child's passport, school reports and any proof of the previous school. If your child has no citizen service number (BSN) yet, the school can use a temporary education number.

Sources: [Rijksoverheid, registering at a secondary school](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/onderwijs/voortgezet-onderwijs/vraag-en-antwoord/voortgezet-onderwijs/inschrijven-voortgezet-onderwijs) · [Rijksoverheid, passend onderwijs](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/vraag-en-antwoord/passend-onderwijs/hoe-krijgt-mijn-kind-passend-onderwijs) · [Rijksoverheid, BSN in education](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/privacy-en-persoonsgegevens/burgerservicenummer-bsn/bsn-in-het-onderwijs)

### 5. Which year group will my child join?

Not by a national rule. The old rule that a child joins group 3 depending on a 1 October birthday ended in 1985. Some schools still use a similar guide. Today the school decides, together with you, and looks at age, development and earlier schooling. Children usually start in group 1 around the age of four and are in group 8 at about age 11. For a child who arrives from abroad, the school decides which year fits. Ask how the school makes this decision, and what extra help your child gets for Dutch. The decision to move up or repeat a year is also the school's.

Sources: [Rijksoverheid, who decides whether my child repeats a year](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/onderwijs/basisonderwijs/vraag-en-antwoord/basisonderwijs/wie-bepaalt-of-mijn-kind-blijft-zitten-of-overgaat-naar-de-volgende-klas) · [Rijksoverheid, when may my child start primary school](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/onderwijs/basisonderwijs/vraag-en-antwoord/basisonderwijs/wanneer-mag-mijn-kind-naar-de-basisschool)

### 6. When must my child go to school?

A child may start at age 4. School is compulsory (leerplicht) from the first day of the month after the fifth birthday. It applies to every child who lives in the Netherlands, whatever the nationality. Compulsory attendance ends at the end of the school year in which the child turns 16. After that, young people without a havo, vwo or mbo level 2 diploma must keep studying until they are 18 (kwalificatieplicht). If you live in the Netherlands but your child goes to a school abroad, for example in Belgium or Germany, you can ask your municipality's truancy officer for an exemption. You then report every year that your child attends school abroad.

Sources: [Rijksoverheid, leerplicht en kwalificatieplicht](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/leerplicht/leerplicht-en-kwalificatieplicht) · [Rijksoverheid, compulsory education and a school abroad](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/leerplicht/vraag-en-antwoord/leerplicht-buitenlandse-school) · [Rijksoverheid, when may my child start primary school](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/onderwijs/basisonderwijs/vraag-en-antwoord/basisonderwijs/wanneer-mag-mijn-kind-naar-de-basisschool)

### 7. Can we take a holiday outside the school holidays?

Normally no, once your child is subject to compulsory education. The school head can give extra leave only if the nature of your work means you cannot take a family holiday in any school holiday, for example seasonal work. It is for up to 10 days, once per school year. Apply at least 8 weeks before the trip. Leave is not possible in the first two weeks of the school year. If you take your child out without permission, you can be fined. The Rijksoverheid does not give an amount, so ask your municipality's truancy officer. A child of 4 is not yet subject to compulsory attendance, but ask the school what it expects. Longer absences are not allowed under this rule.

Sources: [Rijksoverheid, leerplicht and school holidays](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/leerplicht/vraag-en-antwoord/leerplicht-schoolvakanties)

### 8. Is Dutch school free? What is the ouderbijdrage?

State-funded schools do not charge tuition. Many ask for a voluntary parental contribution (ouderbijdrage) for extras such as trips, sports days or celebrations. It is voluntary. Since a law of August 2021, a school may not exclude your child from activities because you did not pay, and may not offer a free replacement activity. The school guide should say this. You still pay for things that are not part of school, such as a private after-school club or lunch supervision. Some families report pressure in practice. If that happens, point to the school guide and the Rijksoverheid information, and contact the parent council or the Rijksoverheid information line.

Sources: [Rijksoverheid, voluntary parental contribution](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/2023/09/01/documenten-vrijwillige-ouderbijdrage-po-en-vo) · [Inspectie, voluntary parental contribution report 2023](https://www.onderwijsinspectie.nl/binaries/onderwijsinspectie/documenten/rapporten/2023/04/20/voldoen-scholen-aan-de-wetgeving-omtrent-vrijwillige-ouderbijdrage/Voldoen+scholen+aan+de+wetgeving+omtrent+vrijwillige+ouderbijdrage-Feiten+en+reflectie.pdf)

### 9. What are school hours and what happens at lunch?

Hours differ per school. Some primary schools have a continuous schedule (continurooster): all children stay at school during lunch, teachers supervise, and parents do not pay a lunch fee, although a voluntary contribution can be requested. Other schools have a break at midday. Then children go home or stay for lunch supervision (overblijven or tussenschoolse opvang, TSO). The school must organise this on request, and parents pay a contribution. The parent council must agree with the amount. Check the school guide for the weekly schedule, the lunch rules and the cost.

Sources: [Rijksoverheid, lunch at primary school](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/basisonderwijs/vraag-en-antwoord/kan-mijn-kind-tussen-de-middag-overblijven-op-de-basisschool)

### 10. What about after-school care and childcare benefit?

After-school care (BSO) is separate from school. A BSO provider collects children from school and keeps them until parents finish work. You pay the provider. The state can pay part of the cost through the childcare benefit (kinderopvangtoeslag), paid by the Belastingdienst. In 2026 the maximum hourly rate for BSO is 9.98 euro and the maximum benefit is 96 percent for lower incomes. The percentage falls as income rises. The rules change from year to year, and conditions apply to your work, income and residence. Lunch supervision at school (TSO) does not count as childcare for the benefit. Check the current rules at the Belastingdienst before you sign a contract.

Sources: [Rijksoverheid, childcare benefit amounts 2026](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/belastingen-uitkeringen-en-toeslagen/kinderopvangtoeslag/bedragen-kinderopvangtoeslag-2026) · [Rijksoverheid, lunch at primary school](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/basisonderwijs/vraag-en-antwoord/kan-mijn-kind-tussen-de-middag-overblijven-op-de-basisschool)

### 11. How does the school advice work, and can I change it?

At the end of group 8 the school gives a school advice (schooladvies), based on your child's whole record. It can name one level or two, for example havo and vwo. In January the school gives a provisional advice. Your child then takes the end-of-primary test. If the test advice is higher, the school adjusts the advice upward, unless it decides this is not in your child's interest, and then it must explain why. If the test result is more than half a level higher, the school may choose a level in between. The school gives the final advice by 24 March 2027. A secondary school must place your child at least at the advised level, and may not require a one-level advice. The test cannot lower the advice.

Sources: [Rijksoverheid, admission based on the final school advice](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/onderwijs/schooladvies-en-doorstroomtoets-basisschool/toelating-voortgezet-onderwijs-gebaseerd-op-definitief-schooladvies) · [Rijksoverheid, timeline school advice 2027](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/schooladvies-en-doorstroomtoets-basisschool/tijdlijn-schooladvies-2027)

### 12. Can I choose any primary school?

You can register at any school, and at more than one. There is no national catchment area. A school can be full, have a waiting list, or give priority to certain children. Some municipalities have a central procedure. In Amsterdam, for example, priority goes first to a brother or sister already at the school, then to children from a linked playgroup, children of staff and children who live close by. A lottery decides when there are more applicants than places. The school's admission policy is public, usually in the school guide or on its website. A public school is open to every child. Ask each school how it handles registration and waiting lists.

Sources: [Rijksoverheid, when to register at a primary school](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/vraag-en-antwoord/basisonderwijs/wanneer-kind-aanmelden-basisschool) · [OCO Amsterdam, priority rules for primary schools](https://ocoamsterdam.nl/vragen/voorrangsregels-voor-plaatsen-van-kinderen-op-basisscholen-in-amsterdam)

### 13. Can I choose any secondary school? Is there a catchment area?

You can register at any secondary school that offers the advised level. You may register at more than one, because a school can be full. A school may refuse a child only on limited grounds, such as having no places left. It may not use extra tests or IQ scores. A bilingual (tto) or top-sport school may test only for its special skills. Some regions have agreements between schools on how pupils are spread, and places can be given by lottery. Rules differ per region. Registration is usually in the sign-up week at the end of March. Ask your child's primary school how your region works.

Sources: [Rijksoverheid, registering at a secondary school](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/onderwijs/voortgezet-onderwijs/vraag-en-antwoord/voortgezet-onderwijs/inschrijven-voortgezet-onderwijs) · [Rijksoverheid, admission based on the final school advice](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/onderwijs/schooladvies-en-doorstroomtoets-basisschool/toelating-voortgezet-onderwijs-gebaseerd-op-definitief-schooladvies)

### 14. Which cities use a lottery for secondary school?

Several large regions do, and the rules differ. Amsterdam places all pupils in one central round with a random lot number and a ranked list of schools. Taking part is mandatory there. In the Utrecht regions a lottery is held at schools that have more first-choice applications than places. In 2026 this happened at twelve schools. In Haaglanden eight schools drew lots in 2026. Rotterdam and Eindhoven have their own procedures. We could not confirm the rules for Haarlem or Nijmegen. Procedures can change every year, so read the current guide of your region and ask your child's primary school.

Sources: [De VO Gids, how lotteries work](https://www.devogids.nl/stappenplan-vo-gids/hulp/hoe-werkt-loting-voor-de-middelbare-school/) (secondary source) · [Samen naar het VO, Utrecht 2026](https://www.samennaarhetvo.nl/aktueel/persbericht-2026/) · [BOVO Haaglanden, results 2026](https://www.bovohaaglanden.nl/uitslag-aanmeldingen-middelbare-school-haaglanden-2026/) · [OCO Amsterdam](https://ocoamsterdam.nl/)

### 15. My child needs extra support. What are my rights?

Dutch schools have a duty of care (zorgplicht). The school where you register your child must offer a suitable place, in the school itself or at another school. Schools work together in regional partnerships (samenwerkingsverbanden passend onderwijs). Register in writing at least 10 weeks before the new school year and tell the school about the support your child needs. The school has 6 weeks to decide and can extend this once by up to 4 weeks. If you disagree with the plan, you can involve your regional partnership. Special primary schools (sbo) and special schools need a formal declaration of eligibility (toelaatbaarheidsverklaring) first. A practical education (praktijkonderwijs) advice needs one too.

Sources: [Rijksoverheid, how does my child get passend onderwijs](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/vraag-en-antwoord/passend-onderwijs/hoe-krijgt-mijn-kind-passend-onderwijs) · [Rijksoverheid, passend onderwijs](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/passend-onderwijs) · [Rijksoverheid, choosing a primary school](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/vraag-en-antwoord/basisonderwijs/basisschool-kiezen-voor-kind)

### 16. Can a religious school refuse my child?

A public school is open to every child, and its lessons are not based on a religion. A bijzondere (special) school teaches from a religion, a belief or a teaching concept, such as Montessori. The Rijksoverheid says a special school may require pupils and teachers to hold a religion or belief. This applies only where it relates to the school's foundation. A school may not discriminate, for example on origin. A court decides in a dispute. Read the school guide and ask about the admission policy before you register.

Sources: [Rijksoverheid, public and special education](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/onderwijs/vrijheid-van-onderwijs/openbaar-en-bijzonder-onderwijs) · [Rijksoverheid, choosing a primary school](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/vraag-en-antwoord/basisonderwijs/basisschool-kiezen-voor-kind)

### 17. I have the 30% ruling. Does that change school choice?

This site gives no tax advice. In general, employers can sometimes pay school fees for an international school tax-free as extraterritorial costs, under conditions set by the Belastingdienst. The conditions include the type of school and who it is meant for. The 30% ruling is planned to become a 27% ruling from 2027, and the salary conditions change. Do not choose a school only because of a tax rule. Ask your employer's HR or a tax adviser before you sign a contract with a school, and read the current Belastingdienst pages. State-funded Dutch schools charge no tuition, so there is nothing to reimburse for those.

Sources: [Belastingdienst, content of the expat scheme](https://www.belastingdienst.nl/wps/wcm/connect/bldcontentnl/belastingdienst/zakelijk/internationaal/personeel/u_bent_niet_in_nederland_gevestigd_loonheffingen_inhouden/als_u_loonheffingen_gaat_inhouden/extraterritoriale_kosten_en_de_30procentregeling/inhoud_van_de_regeling/inhoud_van_de_regeling) · [Business.gov.nl, 30% ruling down to 27%](https://business.gov.nl/amendments/30-percent-ruling-compensation-down-to-27-percent/)

### 18. Will my child's foreign school diplomas be recognised?

For study in the Netherlands, Nuffic compares a foreign diploma with a Dutch one. This is called a credential evaluation. It covers higher education and almost all secondary diplomas. It is not binding. Dutch universities and universities of applied sciences decide who they admit, and they can add their own requirements, such as subjects or a language test. Ask the programme you want before you pay for an evaluation. For school places, ask the school what documents it wants. Keep copies of reports and diplomas, with translations if needed.

Sources: [Nuffic, applying for a credential evaluation](https://www.nuffic.nl/en/study-and-work-in-the-netherlands/applying-for-a-credential-evaluation) · [Study in NL, checking your diploma](https://www.studyinnl.org/plan-your-stay/checking-your-diploma)

### 19. Can my child study in English at university?

Yes, many programmes at Dutch universities and universities of applied sciences are taught in English. Study in NL keeps a database of English-taught programmes. Entry still depends on your diploma. A vwo diploma or a completed first year of hbo (propedeuse) gives admission to a research university, and a havo or mbo level 4 diploma gives access to hbo. Some programmes ask for particular subjects, a language test or selection. For a foreign secondary diploma, check the requirements of the programme, and ask for a Nuffic credential evaluation if the institution wants one.
Sources: [Study in NL](https://www.studyinnl.org/) · [Rijksoverheid, which diplomas give access to university or hbo](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/vraag-en-antwoord/hoger-onderwijs/met-welke-diploma-s-kan-ik-naar-de-universiteit-of-hogeschool) · [Nuffic, education in the Netherlands](https://www.nuffic.nl/en/education-systems/netherlands)

### 20. How should I read the numbers on this site?

The numbers are context for a talk with the school, not a ranking. Pupil counts, school advice, exam results and distances come from DUO open data. To protect privacy, DUO hides groups of fewer than 5 pupils, so a blank or "fewer than 5" means the group is hidden. Small groups also make percentages swing a lot. A school advice mix describes the pupils a school has. It does not say how good the school is. Exam pass rates depend on who the school admits. Inspection judgements come from the Inspectie van het Onderwijs. A school without a judgement has simply not been rated yet, and that is not a bad sign. The site never sorts schools by best.

Sources: [DUO open education data](https://duo.nl/open_onderwijsdata/) · [Inspectie van het Onderwijs](https://www.onderwijsinspectie.nl/)

### 21. Do I need a BSN or an address before I can register?

You register your child yourself and the school asks for your child's citizen service number (BSN). If your child has no BSN yet, the school can enrol your child with a temporary education number (onderwijsnummer), which DUO issues. Tell the school when your child gets a BSN. If you live in the Netherlands, you get a BSN by registering with your municipality. Bring a passport, proof of address and de-registration or school reports from the previous school. In municipalities with a central procedure, such as Amsterdam, a proof of the future home address can be required. Ask the school what it needs, and start early.

Sources: [Rijksoverheid, BSN in education](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/onderwerpen/privacy-en-persoonsgegevens/burgerservicenummer-bsn/bsn-in-het-onderwijs) · [Rijksoverheid, registering at a secondary school](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/onderwijs/voortgezet-onderwijs/vraag-en-antwoord/voortgezet-onderwijs/inschrijven-voortgezet-onderwijs)

## B. Note for the route stop "Before school: choose and register"

Choose and register on time. Popular schools and concept schools (Montessori, Dalton, Jenaplan, Steiner, bilingual primaries) can have long local waiting lists, so check each school's registration rules early. You can register from age 3, not earlier, and at least 10 weeks before the fourth birthday. You may register at more than one school. Some schools keep their own early lists. Ask what such a list means at that school. In some cities there is a central procedure. In Amsterdam the municipality sends a letter and a form around the third birthday. You hand it in at your first-choice school by a deadline that depends on the birth date, and places follow priority rules and a lottery.

Sources: [Rijksoverheid, when to register](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/vraag-en-antwoord/basisonderwijs/wanneer-kind-aanmelden-basisschool) · [OCO Amsterdam, when to register](https://ocoamsterdam.nl/vragen/wanneer-moet-ik-mijn-kind-aanmelden-voor-de-basisschool) · [OCO Amsterdam, priority rules](https://ocoamsterdam.nl/vragen/voorrangsregels-voor-plaatsen-van-kinderen-op-basisscholen-in-amsterdam)

## C. Note for the route stop "Year group 8: advice, test and sign-up"

This is not fully covered on the page today. The dates are right, but open days and city lotteries are not mentioned.

Act on time. Open days are usually in the winter, often January and February, and dates differ per school, so make a short list in the autumn. Sign up in the national sign-up week, 25 to 31 March 2027. Placements start from 1 April 2027. Cities such as Amsterdam, Utrecht and The Hague use central procedures with a lottery, so check your region's guide early and ask your child's school.

## D. tto label and glossary entry

Label (one line): Bilingual (tto): secondary, at least half in English

Glossary entry:

**tto (tweetalig onderwijs):** bilingual secondary education, almost always Dutch and English. In the first years of havo and vwo at least half of the lessons are taught in English (30 percent in vmbo). Pupils take the normal Dutch exams and also get a certificate for the extra language skill.

Note on language: Nuffic's information on secondary tto describes English. German and French are mentioned only for the bilingual primary pilot, and we could not confirm a Dutch-German tto secondary school.

Sources: [Rijksoverheid, what is tto](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/vraag-en-antwoord/onderwijs-en-internationalisering/wat-is-tweetalig-onderwijs-tto-in-het-voortgezet-onderwijs) · [Nuffic, bilingual education](https://www.nuffic.nl/onderwijssectoren/voortgezet-onderwijs/tweetalig-onderwijs) · [Nuffic, all tto schools](https://www.nuffic.nl/onderwijssectoren/voortgezet-onderwijs/tweetalig-onderwijs/alle-tto-scholen-in-nederland)
